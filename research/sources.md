# Research and source notes

Research checked September 13, 2026. These comics are fictional satire informed by the following reports and discussions.

## AI Middle Manager

- **Source:** IBM, [Vibing fatigue: Navigating the hidden cognitive costs of vibe coding](https://www.ibm.com/think/insights/navigating-the-hidden-cost-cognitive-cost-vibe-coding).
- **Published:** September 11, 2026.
- **Verified premise:** The article describes the burden of steering, interpreting, and verifying AI outputs; managing concurrent agents; repeated permission requests; and a shift from implementation to management.
- **Comic invention:** The specific four bot notifications and the line “I invented middle management.”
- **Additional current discussion:** [Hacker News: Multi-agent workflows in production; Where people using 1000s of agents?](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49689454), September 13, 2026. A fresh discussion, not evidence of widespread adoption.

## The $19 Toaster

- **Source:** The Atlantic, [It's All Fun and Games Until You Give AI Your Credit Card](https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/2026/09/instinct-ai-personal-assistant-credit-card/688607/).
- **Published:** September 13, 2026.
- **Verified premise:** Personal agents act on users' behalf in the real world, including shopping and dealing with vendors, with consequences when they make mistakes.
- **Comic invention:** The toaster, the $247 in AI fees, and the $3 saving. These are not an actual reported incident or product prices.

## Human Power Supply

- **Source:** Rest of World, [Chinese businesses are giving away AI tokens with coffee, credit cards, and dumplings](https://restofworld.org/2026/china-ai-tokens-consumer-rewards-credit-cards-telcos-deepseek/).
- **Published:** September 4, 2026; surfaced on Hacker News on September 9.
- **Verified premise:** Businesses in China offer AI tokens as customer incentives, including coffee and food promotions.
- **Comic invention:** A developer repeatedly buying and drinking coffee to let their AI finish a task.

## Certified Slop

- **Source:** TechCrunch, [Apple has a new way to prove your iPhone photos aren't AI slop](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/apple-has-a-new-way-prove-your-iphone-photos-arent-ai-slop/).
- **Published:** September 9, 2026.
- **Verified premise:** The report describes image-provenance features based on signed sensor data and a reference image.
- **Comic invention:** A fictional, unbranded phone authenticating a photo of an invented six-legged moon cat. The joke distinguishes camera capture from a truthful subject; it does not report a tested flaw in Apple's implementation.
